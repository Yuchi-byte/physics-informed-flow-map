0002 - Full-waveform inversion by inference-time tilting

Proof-of-concept · 2026-06-25 · physics-informed-flow-map · see also research plan · prior work

Can we invert a subsurface velocity map from seismic data by tilting a generative flow prior toward the measurements - physics at inference, not training? This PoC says yes.

Result. DPS-style guided sampling of our FlatVel-A flow prior toward simulated seismic data drove the data misfit to 0.5% of an unguided sample and recovered a plausible flat-layer velocity map (best-of-4 posterior MAE ≈ 300 m/s). The thesis - flow prior + inference-time tilting - holds.

Method

Posterior sampling of $p(v \mid d) \propto p(v)\, \exp(-\tfrac{1}{2\sigma^2}\, \lVert F(v)-d \rVert^2)$. We run the prior's generative ODE noise→velocity and, at each step, estimate the clean map $\hat v = x_t + (1-t)\, v_\theta$, score its data misfit through the differentiable forward operator $F$ (Deepwave, M1), and nudge the state down that gradient:

$$x \leftarrow x + \Delta t\, v_\theta - \zeta\, \nabla_x \lVert F(\hat v)-d \rVert^2 .$$

  • Prior: the tuned FlatVel-A flow (DiT 256/6/8 @ 64×64, EMA).
  • Forward operator: physics.forward.simulate - Deepwave acoustic wave equation, OpenFWI geometry (70×70 @10 m, 5 sources, 70 receivers, 15 Hz Ricker, 1001×1 ms).
  • Bridge: prior sample (64×64, $[-1,1]$) → resize 70×70 → denormalize to m/s → $F$, differentiable end-to-end.
  • Sampler: physics.tilt.guided_sample - model- and physics-agnostic (flow and $F$ passed as callables).

Tuning the tilt

Raw constant guidance barely worked (≤11% misfit reduction even at $\zeta=10^{-6}$): the gradient magnitude is wildly scale-dependent. Normalizing each correction to unit norm (so $\zeta$ is a state-space step size) fixed it - the misfit then falls monotonically with $\zeta$:

Reading the result

  • It fits the data (misfit → 0.5%) and stays on the velocity manifold - a plausible flat-layer model, not data-fitting noise. The prior is doing its job.
  • Posterior diversity: 4 samples spanned MAE 297-1239 m/s - genuine uncertainty, which point-estimate inverters can't give.
  • The middle layer is the error - classic FWI non-uniqueness / depth resolution: data-fit ≠ unique velocity, worsened by limited aperture (5 sources) and the 64→70 resolution slack.
